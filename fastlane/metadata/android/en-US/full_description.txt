You are skipper of the Godspeed Ginny for McRiver Cruises & Cargo. The booking is Nowhere—three days downriver. You will be back at Cra-Zi Landing before dark. Reverse is unreliable. The safety valve is painted shut.

Godspeed Ginny is a skipper comedy of bad charts, worse freight, and a riverboat that will not reverse. Your job is not only to arrive. Your job is to keep the room laughing while the boiler climbs, the hull groans, and the chart tells cheerful lies.

THE SKIPPER IS THE ENTERTAINMENT
Passengers paid for a tour. What they get is you. Spend scarce beats on patter, steering, wrench work, or bailing—every choice is a trade. Entertain too hard and the boiler runs hot. Stay quiet and the room goes cold. The gauges do not care about your timing.

THE CHART LIES
The river was surveyed once, by one cartographer, some years ago. Forks look safe until they are not. Landmarks shuffle. Hazards wait where the pencil said “scenic.” Annotate what you learn. Next cruise, your notes are how you beat the map.

BOOK FREIGHT. CAST OFF.
Pick cargo at the landing, take your purse, and shove off. Runs shuffle sights, forks, and river stretches so no two cruises feel identical. Salvage material. Keep a leopard story if you must. Come home and settle the books.

WHY PLAY
• Short river trials built for a phone in your hand
• Comedy first—jokes, chart lies, and McRiver bureaucracy
• Real pressure: boiler, water, hull, and “the room”
• Career memory: purse, annotations, salvage, and bragging rights
• Offline play; crash reporting only to help us keep the boat afloat

TIPS FOR NEW SKIPPERS
Read the room before you empty the joke book. Steer when the stretch actually threatens. Leave a beat for the boiler. Trust the chart only as far as you have annotated it. Nowhere is always three days away. You always come back to Crazy.
